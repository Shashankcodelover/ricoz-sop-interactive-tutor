Build against the approved baseline: All committed work must trace to the Gate G2-approved MVP scope or an

operational configurations.

approval.

2.37 PHASE 03 — DEFECT & ISSUE MANAGEMENT SOP
2.37.1 Defect Severity Classification
Severity 1 — Critical
A defect creates a serious security, data integrity, customer safety or core-service failure, or makes an essential user
journey unusable without a viable workaround.
Required Action: Immediate escalation, containment assessment and prioritized remediation.
Severity 2 — High
A major function is impaired, or a critical customer journey is materially degraded.
Required Action: Prioritized remediation before launch approval unless a formally accepted, safe and compliant
exception is possible.
Severity 3 — Medium
A noncritical function is affected, with limited business impact or a viable workaround.
Required Action: Assign a correction owner and target date based on risk.
Severity 4 — Low
A minor cosmetic or low-impact issue that does not materially affect the intended customer outcome.
Required Action: Record and prioritize within the approved backlog.

2.37.3 Defect Closure Procedure
1. QA or the reporting owner records the issue.
2. Engineering reviews and confirms reproducibility.
3. Severity and priority are assigned.
4. A correction owner is appointed.
5. Engineering implements the fix.
6. Required code review and automated checks are completed.
7. QA or the authorized verifier retests the affected behavior.
8. Relevant regression tests are executed.
9. The defect is closed only after successful verification.

2.40 PHASE PURPOSE & EXPECTED OUTCOMES
Phase 4 verifies whether the integrated MVP can perform its intended functions under realistic conditions and whether
the supporting business organization is ready for controlled public launch.
The phase begins with the release candidate produced during Phase 3. All subsequent testing, fixes and configuration
changes must be traceable to an identified build version.
Unlike development, where the primary objective is implementing functionality, validation focuses on detecting failures,
verifying requirements, assessing customer experience, confirming operational reliability and producing credible
launch-readiness evidence.
2.40.1 Primary Objectives
1. Validate the approved MVP against functional and nonfunctional requirements.
2. Execute end-to-end functional, integration and regression testing.
3. Conduct user acceptance testing with representative users or authorized business representatives.
4. Assess relevant security, privacy, accessibility, performance and reliability risks.
5. Verify the product's critical transaction, authentication and customer data flows.
6. Validate landing pages, conversion tracking, campaign attribution and customer acquisition workflows.
7. Test customer onboarding, support, commercial operations and exception handling.
8. Verify that launch-critical personnel understand their responsibilities.
9. Resolve or formally assess outstanding defects and readiness gaps.
10. Produce a consolidated readiness assessment for Gate G4.
2.40.2 Phase Execution Rules
Rule 1 — No untracked changes. Every code, configuration or customer-facing change must be recorded and
associated with the relevant release candidate.
Rule 2 — Evidence before closure. A test or defect cannot be marked passed or closed without documented
verification evidence.
Rule 3 — Independent validation. Wherever practical, the person verifying a critical feature should be different from
the person who implemented it.
Rule 4 — Risk-based coverage. Testing must prioritize core customer journeys, payments, identity, sensitive data,
external integrations and other high-impact functions relevant to the product.
Rule 5 — Production-like testing. Use a controlled environment that reasonably represents production configurations,
permissions, integrations and deployment behavior.

-- 110 of 211 --

Rule 6 — Protect real customer information. Use synthetic or appropriately anonymized test data unless a separate
authorized process permits otherwise.
Rule 7 — No score-based safety override. Mandatory security, privacy, legal, financial and operational controls cannot
be bypassed through readiness scoring.
Rule 8 — Launch remains conditional. Gate G4 supports the Day 24 Go/No-Go decision but does not replace it.

Severity 1 — Critical
A serious security, data integrity, customer safety or essential service failure, or a complete failure of a core journey

Severity 2 — High
A major functional or operational failure with substantial customer or business impact.
Severity 3 — Medium
A limited functional problem with manageable impact and an acceptable workaround.
Severity 4 — Low
A minor cosmetic or low-impact issue.
Severity describes impact. Priority determines correction order and may additionally consider urgency, frequency,
exposure and dependencies.

2.49.2 Defect Lifecycle
New → Triaged → Assigned → In Progress → Ready for Retest → Verified → Closed

3. Verify the expected result.
4. Test relevant neighboring functionality.

2.52 PHASE 04 — COMMON RISKS & CONTROL ACTIONS

Impact: Previously working functionality becomes unstable.
Control: Require targeted retesting and regression testing after relevant changes.
Risk 3 — Security or Performance Testing Is Insufficient
Impact: Serious risks may remain undiscovered before launch.
Control: Use risk-based specialist assessments and extend validation where necessary.
Risk 4 — UAT Participants Are Unavailable
Impact: Business acceptance cannot be established.
Control: Confirm participants early and maintain approved backup coverage.

Impact: Campaign performance cannot be measured reliably.
Control: Test conversion events, attribution and lead routing using controlled scenarios.
Risk 6 — Operational Procedures Fail During Realistic Use
Impact: Customers experience delays, unresolved issues or inconsistent service.
Control: Conduct end-to-end dry runs and incident simulations.

Impact: The organization launches an unstable or noncompliant product.
